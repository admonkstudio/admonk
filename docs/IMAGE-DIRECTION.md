# Admonk — Image Direction

This document defines how photography, AI-generated imagery, mockups, 3D visuals, and other image-based assets should be created and selected for Admonk web experiences.

Images are not decoration.

They are part of the design system and should contribute to:

* Identity
* Emotion
* Storytelling
* Composition
* Perceived quality
* Differentiation
* Product understanding
* Brand aspiration

The core principle is:

> **Every image should have a job.**

---

# 1. Image Philosophy

Admonk is visually led.

A web experience should not rely on typography alone to create character.

Imagery can carry:

* Atmosphere
* Brand personality
* Product value
* Context
* Human emotion
* Depth
* Materiality
* Visual storytelling

A strong image should make the page feel designed before the visitor reads the copy.

---

# 2. Primary Principle

> **Art-direct the image for the website. Do not design the website around whatever image happens to exist.**

Before creating or selecting an image, understand:

* Where it will appear.
* What it needs to communicate.
* What sits beside it.
* Where text needs to go.
* How it will crop.
* How it should behave on mobile.
* Whether it will move.
* What visual role it plays in the overall composition.

---

# 3. Images Should Serve the Concept

Do not begin with:

> "We need a cool image here."

Begin with:

> "What should the visitor feel or understand at this moment?"

Then determine whether the best visual is:

* Photography
* Product mockup
* 3D
* CGI
* Illustration
* Interface
* Diagram
* Abstract composition
* Human portrait
* Environmental scene
* AI-generated photography
* Motion graphic

The format follows the concept.

---

# 4. Preferred Image Character

Admonk generally responds strongly to imagery that feels:

* Cinematic
* Editorial
* Controlled
* Realistic
* Art-directed
* Premium
* Atmospheric
* Deep
* Intentional

This often includes:

* Strong light and shadow
* Deep blacks
* Carefully controlled highlights
* Brand-colored light
* Interesting framing
* Large negative space
* Realistic materials
* Strong depth
* Deliberate perspective

---

# 5. Realism

For AI-generated imagery, the default preference is:

> **Photographic realism.**

The image should feel like it could have been produced through:

* Photography
* Studio lighting
* High-end commercial production
* Editorial art direction
* Product photography

Even surreal concepts should often retain believable:

* Skin
* Materials
* Light
* Shadows
* Depth
* Reflections
* Camera behavior

---

# 6. Surrealism

Surreal imagery is welcome when it communicates an idea.

Examples:

* A digital interface existing as a physical object.
* A person interacting with impossible transparent technology.
* A product appearing in an unexpected environment.
* A familiar object transformed to represent a concept.
* Physical materials representing digital systems.

The goal is:

> **Impossible concept, believable execution.**

Avoid surrealism that exists only to appear strange.

---

# 7. Cinematic Photography

Cinematic imagery may use:

* Directional lighting
* Deep shadows
* Controlled highlights
* Low-key environments
* Strong color contrast
* Atmospheric haze
* Selective focus
* Large areas of darkness
* Dramatic framing

The result should feel sophisticated rather than theatrical.

---

# 8. Editorial Photography

Editorial photography is especially useful when the objective is:

* Brand character
* Lifestyle
* Human presence
* Cultural positioning
* Premium perception

Editorial images may use:

* Unusual crops
* Partial faces
* Hands
* Silhouettes
* Strong negative space
* Off-center composition
* Controlled asymmetry
* Dramatic close-ups

---

# 9. Human Presence

People should not automatically be photographed as standard corporate portraits.

Human presence may be communicated through:

* Hands
* Eyes
* Partial faces
* Gestures
* Silhouettes
* Movement
* Reflections
* Close crops
* Interaction with products

These often create stronger visual storytelling than a centered smiling person looking directly at camera.

---

# 10. Avoid Generic Stock Photography

Avoid images such as:

* People shaking hands.
* Teams pointing at screens.
* Generic office meetings.
* Artificially diverse groups smiling at camera.
* People celebrating beside laptops.
* Generic call-center imagery.
* Unrelated lifestyle photography.

If an image could belong to almost any company, it is unlikely to strengthen the identity.

---

# 11. Product and Device Mockups

Mockups are particularly important to Admonk because they help potential clients imagine:

> **What their own business could look like when presented at this level.**

Mockups should feel like campaign imagery.

Not simple portfolio screenshots.

---

# 12. Mockup Art Direction

When presenting a website or interface, consider:

* Camera angle
* Device orientation
* Lighting
* Materials
* Environment
* Human interaction
* Scale
* Background
* Reflections
* Cropping
* Depth
* Brand color

A website screenshot inside a phone is functional.

A carefully art-directed phone inside a cinematic scene becomes part of the brand experience.

---

# 13. Mockup Formats

Possible presentation formats include:

* Smartphone
* Tablet
* Laptop
* Desktop display
* Smartwatch
* Digital billboard
* Store display
* Building signage
* Poster
* Packaging
* Transparent screen
* Projection
* Physical card
* Glass object
* Architectural surface
* Human-held device
* Floating product

Choose the format according to the story.

---

# 14. Do Not Repeat the Same Mockup Language

Avoid presenting every project through:

> Device centered on background.

The portfolio should vary.

One project may use:

* Human-held mobile interface.

Another:

* Architectural signage.

Another:

* Close-up screen detail.

Another:

* Full website screenshot.

Another:

* 3D branded object.

Another:

* Cinematic brand photography.

Variety creates a stronger overall portfolio.

---

# 15. Showing Digital Work

When showing a website, ask:

> **What is the most compelling way to experience this work?**

Possible approaches:

* Full browser experience
* Device mockup
* Detail crop
* Motion clip
* Scroll sequence
* Environmental mockup
* Side-by-side responsive views
* Human interaction
* Interface floating in physical space

Do not automatically place every interface into the same device.

---

# 16. Composition

Composition is one of the most important factors in image quality.

Consider:

* Subject placement
* Scale
* Balance
* Negative space
* Leading lines
* Perspective
* Foreground
* Background
* Depth
* Cropping

The image should be created for the intended layout.

---

# 17. Negative Space

Negative space is valuable because it can provide:

* Text placement
* Visual focus
* Luxury
* Calm
* Responsive flexibility

When generating imagery for a hero, intentionally create usable empty areas.

Do not rely on cropping to create text space after generation.

---

# 18. Typography-Safe Zones

When an image will contain website copy, define the required clear area before generating it.

For example:

```text id="fsejzv"
SUBJECT
██████████

                      EMPTY SPACE
                      FOR HEADLINE
                      AND CTA
```

Avoid placing important visual details behind text.

---

# 19. Subject Scale

The subject should not always fill the frame.

Smaller subjects can create:

* Atmosphere
* Scale
* Luxury
* Mystery
* Space

Large close-ups can create:

* Intensity
* Detail
* Emotion
* Product emphasis

Choose scale intentionally.

---

# 20. Cropping

Cropping should feel deliberate.

Strong cropping can create:

* Tension
* Depth
* Focus
* Modern editorial character

It is acceptable for:

* Parts of devices
* Faces
* Hands
* Products
* Typography

to extend beyond the frame.

The crop should feel intentional rather than accidental.

---

# 21. Perspective

Camera perspective should support the objective.

Possible perspectives include:

* Eye level
* Low angle
* High angle
* Macro
* Close-up
* Wide environmental
* Isometric
* Three-quarter product view
* Over-the-shoulder
* First-person interaction

Avoid defaulting to centered front-facing objects.

---

# 22. Lens Thinking

When generating photographic imagery, think like a photographer.

Examples:

### Wide Lens

Useful for:

* Environment
* Architecture
* Large scenes
* Immersive context

### Normal Lens

Useful for:

* Natural human perspective
* Balanced product scenes

### Telephoto

Useful for:

* Compression
* Premium portraits
* Subject isolation

### Macro

Useful for:

* Materials
* Product details
* Skin
* Technology
* Texture

The exact focal length is less important than understanding the visual effect.

---

# 23. Depth of Field

Use depth of field intentionally.

Shallow depth may create:

* Focus
* Intimacy
* Premium photography
* Cinematic atmosphere

Deep focus may be preferable for:

* Architecture
* Product systems
* Interface storytelling

Avoid artificial blur simply because it looks photographic.

---

# 24. Lighting

Lighting is often more important than the subject itself.

Define:

* Direction
* Strength
* Temperature
* Contrast
* Color
* Hardness
* Atmosphere

Useful approaches include:

* Soft studio light
* Hard directional light
* Rim light
* Edge lighting
* Colored practical light
* Gradient illumination
* Screen glow
* Backlight
* Low-key lighting

---

# 25. Brand-Colored Light

Brand color can enter imagery through lighting rather than flat backgrounds.

For example:

```text id="y00ua6"
Neutral subject
+
Dark environment
+
Controlled purple light
```

often feels more premium than:

```text id="v4jk93"
Purple background
+
Purple objects
+
Purple interface
+
Purple glow everywhere
```

Use color with restraint.

---

# 26. Dark Imagery

Dark imagery should preserve detail.

Black should not simply mean:

> Everything disappears.

Maintain:

* Material separation
* Edge lighting
* Reflections
* Depth
* Tonal variation

A dark image should feel rich.

Not underexposed.

---

# 27. Light Imagery

Light imagery can still feel premium through:

* Warm off-white
* Soft gradients
* Controlled shadow
* Subtle materials
* Negative space

Avoid sterile white environments unless the brand specifically needs them.

---

# 28. Color Strategy

The default preference is:

> **Neutral visual environment + controlled brand accent.**

However, the concept comes first.

A project may legitimately be:

* Monochromatic
* Highly saturated
* Gradient-driven
* Warm
* Cool
* Dark
* Bright

if the brand requires it.

---

# 29. Materiality

Materials can make digital brands feel tangible.

Possible materials include:

* Glass
* Metal
* Fabric
* Paper
* Plastic
* Rubber
* Chrome
* Stone
* Light
* Liquid
* Transparent acrylic
* Matte surfaces

Material choice should reinforce the brand.

---

# 30. Glass

Glass can communicate:

* Precision
* Transparency
* Technology
* Lightness
* Futurism

Avoid generic glassmorphism.

Use actual material behavior:

* Reflection
* Refraction
* Edge thickness
* Light distortion
* Transparency

when glass is part of the visual concept.

---

# 31. Chrome and Metallic Materials

Metallic materials may communicate:

* Technology
* Performance
* Industrial quality
* Luxury
* Precision

Avoid chrome simply because it is trendy.

Ask whether the material belongs to the brand.

---

# 32. Grain and Texture

Texture can reduce the artificial perfection of digital imagery.

Useful applications include:

* Film grain
* Paper texture
* Surface imperfections
* Material detail
* Atmospheric noise

Texture should be subtle enough that it supports rather than overwhelms the composition.

---

# 33. 3D and CGI

3D is a legitimate primary visual language when the concept supports it.

It can be used for:

* Products
* Abstract metaphors
* Branded objects
* Environments
* Interface representation
* Material exploration
* Technical storytelling

The objective is not:

> "Make this look 3D."

It is:

> "Would spatial form communicate this idea better?"

---

# 34. Realistic 3D

When realism is intended, ensure:

* Materials respond naturally.
* Lighting feels physically coherent.
* Shadows are believable.
* Scale is understandable.
* Reflections match the environment.

Poor realism quickly reduces perceived quality.

---

# 35. Abstract 3D

Abstract objects should represent something.

Examples:

* Growth
* Connection
* Transformation
* Flow
* Complexity
* Precision
* Movement

Avoid meaningless spheres, ribbons, and blobs simply because they fill space.

---

# 36. Interfaces Inside Imagery

Digital interfaces can be integrated into photography.

Examples:

* Interface inside a phone
* Interface projected on glass
* Digital screen in architecture
* Transparent interface held by a hand
* Screen illuminating a face
* Interface embedded into a physical object

This creates a bridge between digital product and physical experience.

---

# 37. Interface Accuracy

When the project interface actually exists, prefer using the real design rather than asking AI to reproduce interface details.

AI is generally better used to create:

* Device
* Environment
* Person
* Lighting
* Composition

Then place the real interface into the image where practical.

This preserves:

* Brand accuracy
* Typography
* UI detail
* Product credibility

---

# 38. Text Inside AI Images

Avoid relying on AI-generated text for important brand content.

Do not generate:

* Logos
* Headlines
* UI copy
* Product labels

inside the image when they need to be accurate.

Create the visual first.

Apply accurate typography or branding afterward.

---

# 39. Logo Accuracy

Do not approximate client logos with AI.

Use the actual logo asset.

If a generated scene requires the logo on:

* Signage
* Device
* Clothing
* Packaging

prefer compositing the real logo afterward when possible.

---

# 40. AI Generation Workflow

Use the following process for important imagery.

## Step 1 — Define the Role

What does this image need to achieve?

Examples:

* Hero image
* Portfolio presentation
* Service illustration
* Background atmosphere
* Conceptual storytelling
* Product mockup

---

## Step 2 — Define the Message

What should the viewer understand or feel?

Examples:

* Precision
* Innovation
* Luxury
* Human connection
* Speed
* Transformation
* Trust

---

## Step 3 — Define Composition

Specify:

* Subject
* Position
* Scale
* Camera angle
* Negative space
* Foreground
* Background

---

## Step 4 — Define Photography

Specify:

* Lighting
* Lens feeling
* Depth of field
* Exposure
* Contrast
* Material treatment
* Color temperature

---

## Step 5 — Define Brand Treatment

Specify:

* Accent color
* Visual mood
* Materials
* Environment
* Styling

---

## Step 6 — Generate

Create the initial image.

---

## Step 7 — Evaluate

Do not ask simply:

> "Does this look good?"

Compare it against the intended objectives.

---

## Step 8 — Iterate Specific Problems

Instead of regenerating everything because the image feels slightly wrong, identify the actual issue:

* Camera too close
* Subject too large
* Light too flat
* Background too busy
* Accent too saturated
* Not enough negative space
* Materials too artificial

Then adjust those variables.

---

# 41. Reference-Image Workflow

When using reference imagery, do not copy it blindly.

Analyze it.

Extract:

### Composition

Where is the subject?

### Camera

How close is the viewer?

### Light

Where does the light come from?

### Color

What dominates?

### Material

What creates the visual richness?

### Mood

What emotion does it create?

### Negative Space

How much empty space exists?

### Brand Relationship

Why does the image make sense for that brand?

Then apply the principles to the new project.

---

# 42. Reference vs Imitation

A reference should answer:

> **What do we like about this?**

Not:

> **How do we make the exact same image with another logo?**

Use references to extract principles.

Build a new visual concept for the client.

---

# 43. Reference Boards

For major projects, create a small visual direction board before generating final imagery.

A board might include:

* 2–3 photography references
* Lighting reference
* Material reference
* Color reference
* Mockup reference
* Composition reference

The references do not need to come from the same project.

The combination defines the desired direction.

---

# 44. Image Consistency

Images across the same project should feel connected.

Consistency may come from:

* Lighting
* Color temperature
* Contrast
* Camera treatment
* Materials
* Grain
* Cropping
* Background
* Accent color
* Subject treatment

Do not generate every image independently.

Build a visual family.

---

# 45. Consistency Without Repetition

Connected does not mean identical.

A project may contain:

```text id="o5pqq2"
Portrait
   ↓
Device mockup
   ↓
Architecture
   ↓
Product detail
```

while maintaining the same:

* Lighting
* Color
* Materiality
* Mood

This creates a richer visual system.

---

# 46. Portfolio Direction

Portfolio imagery should accomplish two things.

## Proof

Show what Admonk actually created.

## Aspiration

Make potential clients imagine what their own brand could become.

This means portfolio presentation should receive almost as much art direction as the original project.

---

# 47. Portfolio Hero Images

A portfolio hero should not merely be:

> Screenshot of homepage.

Instead ask:

> **What single image best captures the identity and experience of this project?**

It may be:

* Website in environment
* Product close-up
* Brand image
* Device
* Human interaction
* Cinematic composition
* Full-page interface

---

# 48. Case Study Rhythm

Case studies should vary imagery.

Potential sequence:

```text id="uazk4x"
Art-directed hero
       ↓
Full website
       ↓
Detail crop
       ↓
Brand photography
       ↓
Mobile mockup
       ↓
Interaction clip
       ↓
Environmental application
```

Avoid endless browser screenshots.

---

# 49. Image and Motion

Image direction should consider motion from the start.

Ask whether an image needs:

* Extra crop area
* Multiple layers
* Transparent background
* Separate foreground/background
* High resolution for zoom
* Room for parallax
* A sequence of states

Do not discover after generation that the visual cannot support the intended animation.

---

# 50. Layered Assets

When motion is planned, generate or prepare imagery in layers where practical.

Examples:

```text id="sz9he4"
BACKGROUND
PERSON
DEVICE
SCREEN
LIGHT
ATMOSPHERE
```

This creates more control during animation.

---

# 51. Responsive Image Direction

Desktop and mobile may require different compositions.

Do not rely entirely on automatic cropping.

A desktop hero may need:

> Subject on right + empty left side.

Mobile may require:

> Subject centered + empty upper area.

Generate separate assets when the concept benefits from it.

---

# 52. Aspect Ratios

Always consider the destination before generating.

Common contexts include:

### Website Hero

Wide landscape.

### Portfolio Card

Project-specific card ratio.

### Mobile Section

Portrait.

### Social Media

Square or vertical.

### Full-Screen Mobile

9:16-style portrait composition.

Do not create the visual first and discover the aspect ratio later.

---

# 53. Resolution

Important images should support:

* High-density displays
* Responsive cropping
* Moderate zooming
* Retina screens

But file size matters.

Generate at adequate quality, then optimize for the web.

---

# 54. Image Optimization

Before production use:

* Use appropriate dimensions.
* Compress files.
* Prefer modern web formats where practical.
* Avoid serving oversized desktop assets to mobile when unnecessary.
* Lazy-load below-the-fold imagery where appropriate.

Image quality should not destroy site performance.

---

# 55. Visual Metaphor

When explaining abstract services, imagery can communicate through metaphor.

For example:

### Connection

A path joining multiple objects.

### Automation

One action triggering a sequence.

### Growth

Expansion or upward progression.

### Transformation

An object changing form.

### Identity

A recognizable visual system applied across environments.

The metaphor should be simple enough to understand quickly.

---

# 56. Avoid Literal Technology Clichés

For AI, automation, and technology, avoid defaulting to:

* Robots
* Human brains made of circuits
* Glowing AI heads
* Futuristic server rooms
* Blue network nodes
* Generic holograms
* Binary code
* Random circuitry

Technology can often be represented more intelligently through:

* Behavior
* Connection
* Transformation
* Interface
* Human interaction
* Data flow

---

# 57. Familiarity

When showing technical concepts, use familiar visual objects where possible.

For example:

* Email
* Message
* Form
* CRM
* Database
* Notification
* Calendar
* Dashboard

Then use art direction to elevate them.

This mirrors Admonk's broader principle:

> **Familiar interaction. Unfamiliar presentation.**

---

# 58. Image Hierarchy

A composition should usually contain one dominant visual idea.

Avoid having:

* Multiple hero subjects
* Too many competing objects
* Multiple accent colors
* Several unrelated visual metaphors

The viewer should know where to look.

---

# 59. Simplicity

A strong image can contain:

> One hand.

> One object.

> One light source.

> One accent color.

and feel more premium than a complex scene.

Do not confuse complexity with art direction.

---

# 60. Detail

Minimal imagery requires excellent detail.

Pay attention to:

* Fingers
* Device edges
* Reflections
* Skin
* Shadows
* Materials
* Logos
* Perspective
* UI alignment
* Object geometry

One obvious generation error can destroy the realism of the entire image.

---

# 61. Human Anatomy

Inspect AI-generated people carefully.

Check:

* Hands
* Fingers
* Eyes
* Teeth
* Ears
* Limbs
* Joints
* Clothing interaction

Do not accept an otherwise beautiful image with obvious anatomical mistakes.

---

# 62. Product Geometry

For devices and manufactured objects, inspect:

* Symmetry
* Buttons
* Screen proportions
* Edges
* Perspective
* Reflections
* Object thickness

Premium product visuals require geometric consistency.

---

# 63. Background Discipline

Backgrounds should support the subject.

Avoid adding unnecessary:

* Objects
* Furniture
* Lights
* Shapes
* Architecture

unless they strengthen the story.

A simple background with excellent lighting can create more impact.

---

# 64. Atmosphere

Atmosphere can be created through:

* Light
* Fog
* Grain
* Shadow
* Reflection
* Depth
* Color

Atmosphere should not reduce clarity.

---

# 65. Visual Restraint

An image does not need:

> Light + glow + gradient + chrome + glass + particles + 3D + fog

all at once.

Choose the techniques that belong to the concept.

The fewer techniques used well, the more confident the image often feels.

---

# 66. Image Review Questions

Before approving an image, ask:

### Purpose

* Why does this image exist?
* What does it communicate?

### Brand

* Does it feel specific to this client?
* Does it support their identity?

### Composition

* Is there one clear focus?
* Is the negative space useful?
* Does the crop work?

### Quality

* Does it feel premium?
* Are materials believable?
* Is lighting intentional?

### Website

* Does text fit around it?
* Does it work responsively?
* Can it support the intended motion?

### AI Quality

* Are hands correct?
* Are objects geometrically coherent?
* Is text or branding distorted?
* Does anything immediately reveal that it was poorly generated?

### Originality

* Is this a deliberate concept?
* Or just another visually impressive AI image?

---

# 67. Image Generation Brief

For important assets, define the brief using:

```text id="1zb23l"
OBJECTIVE
What must the image communicate?

SUBJECT
What is shown?

COMPOSITION
Where is everything positioned?

CAMERA
How is it photographed?

LIGHTING
How is the scene illuminated?

MATERIALS
What should surfaces feel like?

COLOR
What palette/accent is used?

MOOD
What should the viewer feel?

WEBSITE USE
Where will the image appear?

SAFE AREA
Where must text/content remain clear?

MOTION
Will the image need to animate?

AVOID
What should specifically not appear?
```

This is preferable to simply writing:

> "Generate a futuristic premium image."

---

# 68. Example Direction

Instead of:

> Create a futuristic AI hero image.

Use a direction such as:

```text id="7yf1ct"
Objective:
Represent intelligent automation as something tangible and premium.

Subject:
A realistic human hand holding a thin transparent glass interface.

Composition:
Hand enters from lower right. Glass object occupies center-right.
Large clean negative space on left for headline.

Camera:
Editorial commercial photography, close-up, slightly low angle.

Lighting:
Dark charcoal environment with controlled electric-blue edge lighting.

Materials:
Real skin, transparent glass with realistic refraction and subtle metallic edge.

Color:
Primarily black and charcoal with one controlled blue accent.

Mood:
Precise, sophisticated, calm, technologically advanced.

Avoid:
Robots, circuitry, generic holograms, excessive glow, visible AI artifacts.
```

The result is more controllable because the art direction is explicit.

Image direction must follow:

`docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md`

> **Structure creates confidence. Art creates desire.**

and:

> **If the copy disappeared, there should still be a visual idea.**

Imagery is one of the primary tools used to create that idea.

# 70. Relationship With Motion

Image creation should also consider:

`docs/MOTION-LANGUAGE.md`

The strongest web imagery may later:

* Reveal
* Scale
* Transform
* Move through depth
* Connect sections
* React to pointer movement

Art direction and motion direction should therefore be planned together.

---

# 71. What to Avoid

Avoid defaulting to:

* Generic stock imagery
* Generic AI futurism
* Meaningless abstract objects
* Excessive glow
* Random 3D blobs
* Over-saturated scenes
* Unrealistic skin
* Distorted hands
* Incorrect UI
* Fake logos
* AI-generated typography
* Busy backgrounds
* Mockup repetition
* Device-on-gradient-background for every project
* Imagery chosen after the layout is finished
* Images that only exist to fill empty space

These are not absolute bans.

They require a reason.

---

# 72. Final Image Principle

Admonk imagery should not merely make a page:

> **Look better.**

It should make the business:

> **Feel more valuable.**

The ideal image is:

> **Specific enough to belong to the brand.
> Artistic enough to create desire.
> Real enough to believe.
> Controlled enough to feel premium.
> Functional enough to work inside the website.**

And the final question should always be:

> **Does this help the client see their business the way they want the world to see it?**
