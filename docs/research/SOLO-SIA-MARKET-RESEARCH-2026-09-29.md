# 2. Executive conclusion

The category is real and increasingly crowded.

We did **not** invent:
- enterprise AI assistants;
- company knowledge/context graphs;
- personal work graphs;
- process discovery from employee activity;
- agent teams;
- deterministic workflow + agent combinations;
- process blueprints;
- role/function workflow mapping;
- interactive AI-generated artifacts;
- meeting capture/follow-up;
- cross-system actions;
- AI usage credits;
- central governance/control planes.

Several 2026 products now cover substantial portions of this stack.

The closest strategic overlap is **Glean**.

The most important conclusion is:

> **SOLO cannot win by saying “AI that understands your company and has agents.” That is already a market category.**

Potential differentiation must come from the integration and product experience:

1. one coherent company operating environment from solo user to enterprise;
2. one intelligent identity — SIA — rather than a visible cast of bots;
3. predefined **department + role + responsibility operating models**;
4. three starting modes — Adapt to Us / Help Us Build / Start with Standard;
5. deterministic onboarding and diagnostic playbooks;
6. a tested component/workspace vocabulary rather than arbitrary runtime UI;
7. SIA as an interactive operator that can fluidly open reports, maps, people pickers, meetings, approvals and artifacts;
8. the same company model across every human role;
9. persistent company artifacts/relationships rather than isolated chat output;
10. product-led/free entry that can start with one person, not an enterprise deployment project.

# 3. Competitive landscape

## 3.1 Glean — closest overall strategic overlap

### What exists

Glean's current public platform includes:
- Enterprise Graph;
- Personal Graph;
- permission-aware enterprise context;
- 250+ connectors/actions;
- Assistant;
- agents;
- agent orchestration/governance/library;
- interactive artifacts;
- proactive AI;
- Work Execution;
- Glean Transform.

The Enterprise Graph maps relationships across people, content, workflows and applications.

The Personal Graph represents each employee's projects, collaborators and work style.

Glean's context-graph work analyzes activity streams across tools, clusters actions into tasks/projects, identifies collaboration patterns and begins modeling process.

**Glean Transform** goes further:
- builds privacy-safe views of recurring work by role/function;
- maps workflow steps, handoffs and time;
- ranks automation/augmentation opportunities;
- drafts agent/skill blueprints;
- measures post-change impact in an ROI ledger.

Interactive Artifacts can create:
- rich pages;
- dashboards;
- prototypes;
- proposals;
- refreshable work products grounded in enterprise knowledge.

### Why it matters to SOLO

This strongly validates:
- Company Graph;
- Personal/Role Lens;
- process discovery from real work;
- persistent enterprise context;
- interactive artifacts;
- workflow analysis;
- measuring process change.

It also means these ideas cannot be treated as SOLO's unique moat.

### Gaps/opportunities relative to our thesis

Based on public product positioning:
- Glean remains enterprise-first;
- standard setup guidance is measured in weeks, largely because of enterprise connector/security rollout;
- its core experience grew from enterprise search/context;
- Glean Transform is explicitly marked **Coming soon** as of current public material;
- role/function workflow analysis exists, but public material does not show a full predefined role-responsibility operating-model catalog used as the primary onboarding architecture;
- its artifacts are flexible/generative; SOLO can differentiate with a stricter tested SIA component/workspace vocabulary for repeatability and UX consistency;
- SOLO can target **solo → SMB → enterprise** with the same product thesis rather than beginning at enterprise knowledge infrastructure.

### Learn/adapt

1. Treat context/relationship graph quality as infrastructure, not a feature.
2. Build Personal/Role Lens separately from company-wide graph.
3. Infer work from activity only with provenance/privacy controls.
4. Turn discovered workflows into action blueprints.
5. Maintain a before/after ROI ledger.
6. Make artifacts persistent, shareable, searchable and refreshable.
7. Use auto-routing/model routing to reduce AI cost.

## 3.2 Microsoft — context layer + deterministic workflows + agent governance

### What exists

Microsoft **Work IQ** builds semantic understanding of:
- email;
- calendar;
- meetings;
- chats;
- files;
- people;
- collaboration patterns;
- line-of-business systems.

Microsoft states that Work IQ creates a real-time model of how an organization operates.

Copilot Studio increasingly combines:
- event triggers;
- deterministic workflows;
- classification/routing;
- specialist agents;
- branching logic;
- monitoring/testing.

**Agent 365** acts as a central enterprise control plane for agent identity, tools, visibility, governance, security and lifecycle.

### YouTube learning

Official Microsoft 2026 demos show:
- route planning grounded across Dynamics, weather/traffic and Microsoft 365 context;
- structured output generated and then communicated into Teams;
- workflows orchestrating specialist agents;
- classification routing tasks into the correct branch;
- inline testing/monitoring.

### Learn/adapt

1. **Workflow is the backbone; AI is inserted where judgment is useful.**
2. Keep classification/routing separate from execution.
3. Treat tool permission as the real authority boundary.
4. Give every agent/action identity and observable lifecycle.
5. Build cross-system demos around one complete business outcome, not disconnected AI features.

## 3.3 ServiceNow — context + action + governance as one enterprise platform

ServiceNow's 2026 platform positioning is extremely close to the enterprise half of SOLO:
- Context Engine;
- Workflow Data Fabric;
- Otto conversational front door;

Context Engine maps:

- services;
- policies;
- historical decisions;
- identity relationships;
- workflow state.

It applies context and policy to agent decisions and records verifiable traces.

Action Fabric allows third-party AI agents to execute governed ServiceNow actions through MCP/A2A/traditional protocols.

### YouTube learning

ServiceNow's official AI Control Tower video emphasizes:
- one place to see/control scattered agents;
- shared data;
- governance;
- enterprise scale.

### Learn/adapt

1. Context must include policy/decision history, not just documents.
2. Do not centralize every provider dataset; federated/live context can be preferable.
3. Keep an explicit internal operations/control plane.
4. Every consequential action should be traceable and explainable.
5. Separate user-facing SIA simplicity from administrator governance complexity.

## 3.4 Salesforce Agentforce Operations — predetermined processes / blueprints

### What exists

Agentforce Operations uses a reusable hierarchy:
- Blueprint;
- Workflow;
- Stage;
- Task;
- Field.

A blueprint is designed once and can run repeatedly.

Tasks can be assigned to:
- humans;
- teams;
- roles;
- AI agents.

Salesforce also advertises a library of **30+ prebuilt process blueprints** for common work such as onboarding and invoice auditing.

Blueprints can be created from:
- natural language;
- unstructured documents;
- process diagrams.

They are versioned and can evolve without silently breaking existing runs.

### YouTube learning

The official 2026 Agentforce Demo Day emphasizes:
- real deployed use cases rather than concept videos;
- human handoffs;
- multi-agent orchestration;
- production-building/testing practices.

### Learn/adapt

1. SOLO's role/responsibility catalog should resolve into **versioned process templates**, not prose.
2. Separate template definition from each live process run.
3. Explicitly version processes.
4. Assign work to a human, role, team or SIA capability.
5. Allow customers to import an existing SOP/process and turn it into a structured process.
6. Build a curated standard-process library as a product asset.

## 3.5 Notion — one agent manipulating native workspace objects

### What exists

Notion Agent:
- creates/edits databases;
- creates pages;
- builds properties, relations, charts and views;
- searches connected tools;
- takes actions in Slack;
- creates reports;
- schedules meetings;
- personalizes to user workflow.

Notion's meeting scheduling is especially relevant:
- ask the Agent to meet with a teammate;
- it displays ranked meeting times through an **interactive schedule grid**;
- the user can book from the same interaction.

AI Meeting Notes can trigger Custom Agents after the meeting, allowing automatic follow-up such as:
- update tracker;
- send recap;
- create tickets.

Notion also introduced interactive HTML blocks and external agent orchestration.

### Why it matters

Our "review report → ask SIA for meeting → interactive panel → book without leaving context" idea is already validated by a major product.

### Learn/adapt

1. SIA must manipulate native SOLO objects, not just output text.
2. Context panels should appear exactly where work is happening.
3. Meeting → notes → actions should be one continuous lifecycle.
4. Keep agents inside the shared workspace rather than sending users to an agent-builder product.
5. Interactive UI should reduce context switching.

### Opportunity

Notion still asks the customer to construct much of the underlying workspace/database structure.

SOLO's proposed advantage:
- predefined operating model;
- role/responsibility model;
- process archetypes;
- company graph;
- diagnostic onboarding.

# 6. Where SOLO can start ahead

## 6.1 Build role/responsibility ontology before generic agent marketplace

Do not ask:
> What agent do you want to create?

Ask:
> What is your role? What are you responsible for?

Then SOLO already knows:
- expected processes;
- metrics;
- artifacts;
- systems;
- actions;
- relationships;
- diagnostic playbooks.

This is more opinionated and potentially easier for mainstream users.

---

## 6.2 One SIA, hidden specialist execution

Do not recreate a visible office full of bots unless transparency requires it.

The user talks to **SIA**.

SIA may internally route to:
- fast model;
- deep model;
- deterministic workflow;
- domain skill;
- background worker;
- specialist sub-agent;
- provider action.

The user primarily experiences one coherent intelligence.

---

## 6.3 Three onboarding modes from day one

1. **Adapt to Us** — discover and preserve mature custom process.
2. **Help Us Build** — propose a sensible operating system and migration path.
3. **Start with Standard** — activate a proven role/process archetype immediately.

Current competitors usually specialize in one or two of these.

---

## 6.4 Combine expected/configured/observed process

For any important process, preserve:

- **Reference** — how the role/process commonly works.
- **Configured** — how this company says it works.
- **Observed** — what activity evidence shows actually happens.

This enables SIA to identify:
- intentional customization;
- process drift;
- bypassed approval;
- hidden work;
- broken handoffs;
- training/adoption gaps.

This three-model comparison should be a core SOLO differentiator.

---

## 6.5 Make onboarding itself the first analysis

Enterprise competitors often require setup before value.

SOLO should progressively show value while indexing:

- who/what has been found;
- relationships discovered;
- processes inferred;
- confidence/provenance;
- gaps;
- first finding.

For small customers, useful value should appear quickly even while deeper indexing continues.

---

## 6.6 Controlled interactive vocabulary

Glean/Notion/Taskade validate generated interactive artifacts.

SOLO should go further in consistency:

- tested component registry;
- tested workspace patterns;
- typed artifact schemas;
- domain components;
- role playbooks;
- deterministic accessibility;
- deterministic authority/permission handling.

SIA composes approved software rather than improvising an application.

---

## 6.7 Proactive but accountable

Adopt the best pattern seen across Glean, Microsoft, ServiceNow, Salesforce and Notion:

- SIA can notice/propose;
- deterministic workflows execute repeatable process;
- sensitive actions require policy/approval;
- every action produces evidence/receipt;
- SIA can explain what happened.

Avoid "AI said it completed" without an authoritative provider/state receipt.

---

## 6.8 ROI ledger as a native product object

Every improvement plan should be measurable:

Before:
- time;
- errors;
- cost;
- handoffs;
- throughput;
- outcome metric.

After:
- same measures.

SIA should be able to say:
> We changed this process 60 days ago. Here is what actually improved.

This makes SIA valuable to management beyond novelty.

---

## 6.9 Free tier should demonstrate the actual product

Learn from Taskade's free credits and Notion's beta/free exploration.

A SOLO free entry should allow at least:
- one user;
- one company;
- one or several role selections;
- limited connectors;
- limited historical indexing;
- a small SIA credit allowance;
- one complete onboarding/analysis loop;
- one safe action.

Do not make the free tier a passive dashboard.

- context/knowledge → relationships → process → action → interactive artifacts → governance.

- company graph;
- agent teams;
- connected enterprise search;
- process discovery;
- interactive artifacts;
- workflow blueprints;
- AI employees;

The combination of:
- solo-to-enterprise one-product architecture;
- predefined role/responsibility operating model;
- three company maturity modes;
- expected/configured/observed process comparison;
- one SIA identity;
- deterministic role onboarding;
- controlled interactive workspace runtime;
- process + artifact + people + meeting continuity;
- product-led/free entry;
- company-wide cumulative model.

This requires validation.

# 10. Current conclusion

The market is not waiting for us.

Several competitors have already reached pieces we had initially considered future-state capabilities.

That is valuable.

It lets SOLO begin with a 2026 baseline instead of rediscovering the last three years of enterprise-agent product learning.

The goal should not be:

> **Build the same stack with a nicer assistant.**

The stronger target is:

> **Take the best proven context, workflow, governance and artifact patterns already validated by the market, then make them disappear behind a radically simpler role-first operating experience led by SIA.**

That is the current hypothesis to challenge in the full architecture/cost audit.
