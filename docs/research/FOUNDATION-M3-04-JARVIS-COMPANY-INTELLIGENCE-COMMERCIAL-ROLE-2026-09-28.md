# FOUNDATION-M3-04 — Jarvis Company Intelligence Commercial/Product Role

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** RESEARCH COMPLETE — OWNER DECISION PENDING  
**Milestone:** FOUNDATION-M3 — Main Product Master Plan  
**Method:** quick internal scan → two high-value sources → two scenarios → challenge → synthesis  
**Implementation authority:** None

## Internal scan

Locked architecture/product direction:
- Jarvis is the shared intelligent experience layer above subscribed/authorized Admonk capabilities;
- department/product Jarvis uses the same core with domain-scoped context/capabilities;
- Company/Executive Jarvis is the same Jarvis with authorized cross-product context, not a second brain;
- M3-03 classifies Jarvis Company Intelligence as a cross-product add-on;
- specialist products must remain independently valuable.

The commercial question is therefore:
> Should all Jarvis capability be separately monetized, or should product-scoped Jarvis be included while Company Intelligence is the paid cross-product expansion?

## External source 1 — Atlassian Rovo

Atlassian currently includes Rovo AI capabilities with eligible paid Jira, Confluence, Service Collection and Teamwork Collection plans. Rovo is embedded in those product experiences and its usage allowances grow with the subscribed products.

Source:
https://www.atlassian.com/licensing/rovo

Admonk implication:
- embedded AI can strengthen the core product rather than becoming a separate purchase barrier;
- more subscribed products can naturally increase the value/context of the AI layer.

## External source 2 — Microsoft 365 Copilot

Microsoft currently offers a baseline Copilot Chat experience while monetizing the fuller Microsoft 365 Copilot experience as a separate paid license/add-on across Microsoft 365 apps and organizational work data.

Source:
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing/enterprise

Admonk implication:
- there is a valid commercial distinction between baseline embedded intelligence and higher-value cross-suite intelligence/orchestration;
- customers can first buy the core productivity/domain products, then pay separately for deeper organizational AI value.

## Scenario A — Jarvis as a separate paid product everywhere

Customers buy Marketing Hub or Support, then separately buy Jarvis to receive the intelligent experience.

### Strengths
- clear AI revenue line;
- simple metering/pricing concept;
- customers who do not want AI can avoid it.

### Challenge
- weakens M3-01 “one intelligent experience”;
- makes the specialist products feel artificially incomplete;
- creates an adoption/paywall barrier before Jarvis proves its value;
- risks turning Jarvis into another product rather than the interaction layer;
- harder to maintain consistent UX if some users have “modern product” and others only legacy dashboard surfaces.

## Scenario B — Product Jarvis included; Company Intelligence monetized as cross-product add-on

Each specialist product includes its product-scoped Jarvis experience:
- conversation;
- Dynamic Workspace where applicable;
- product-scoped reasoning/context;
- product-scoped governed actions according to permissions;
- product-scoped briefings/help.

Customers pay separately for **Jarvis Company Intelligence** when they want:
- cross-product/company-wide context;
- executive/company Operating Lens;
- cross-domain analysis;
- company-level priority brief;
- cross-product orchestration;
- strategy/objective context across products;
- higher-value cross-product agents/workflows where approved.

Usage/credits may still apply to both included and add-on intelligence according to M2-14; “included” does not mean unlimited cost.

### Strengths
- Jarvis becomes part of product quality, not an optional gimmick;
- specialist products remain modern and differentiated;
- natural expansion story;
- Company Intelligence has a clearer higher-value reason to pay;
- matches M3-01/M3-03 architecture exactly.

### Challenge
- some AI cost is included in base product economics;
- boundary between product-scoped and company-scoped value must be clear;
- cross-product features must not leak into base Jarvis accidentally;
- pricing/credits need discipline.

## Synthesis

Choose **Scenario B**.

Commercial model:

### Included with each specialist product
**Product Jarvis**
- intelligent experience for that subscribed product/domain;
- respects the product's permissions, data and action boundaries;
- improves the core product experience.

### Paid cross-product add-on
**Jarvis Company Intelligence**
- activates company/executive cross-product context and orchestration;
- requires at least one eligible specialist product and becomes more valuable with multiple products;
- does not own domain truth;
- may have its own commercial entitlement and credit/usage allocation.

### Optional future premium AI capabilities
High-cost specialist agents, advanced automation or heavy research modes may later be separately metered/add-ons without turning ordinary Jarvis into a separate product.

## Recommended lock

> **M3-04 — Jarvis Included by Product; Company Intelligence as the Cross-Product Add-on**
>
> Product-scoped Jarvis is part of every subscribed specialist product's core experience and is not sold as a separate product.
>
> Each specialist product grants Jarvis only that product's authorized context, capabilities and governed actions.
>
> **Jarvis Company Intelligence** is the separately entitled cross-product add-on that unlocks authorized company/executive context, cross-domain synthesis and orchestration across subscribed products.
>
> Company Intelligence is the same Jarvis core under a broader authorized Operating Lens—not a second assistant or source of truth.
>
> Base-product Jarvis may have included usage/credit limits; “included” does not mean unmetered.
>
> Future high-cost AI/agent capabilities may be separately packaged or metered when value/cost evidence justifies it.
>
> **The customer should never have to pay extra merely to make a specialist product feel intelligent; they pay extra when intelligence becomes broader than the product they bought.**

**Recommendation:** LOCK Scenario B.
