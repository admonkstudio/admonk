# FOUNDATION-M3-02 — Customer / Organization Archetype & Buying Unit

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** LOCKED — OWNER SELECTED SCENARIO B  
**Milestone:** FOUNDATION-M3 — Main Product Master Plan  
**Method:** quick internal scan → two high-value sources → two scenarios → challenge → synthesis  
**Implementation authority:** None

## Internal scan

Locked M3-01:
> **Specialist-first. Platform-backed. Progressively unified.**

Foundation implications:
- each specialist product must solve a clear department/domain problem independently;
- one shared platform reduces duplicated identity/setup/integration/governance;
- Jarvis becomes more valuable as authorized products connect;
- buying may begin in one function but expansion becomes cross-functional.

Therefore the target customer should have enough organizational/system complexity for the platform multiplier to matter, without requiring the first purchase to be a company-wide transformation.

## External source 1 — Gartner

Gartner's 2025 B2B buying research reports buying groups of roughly **5–16 people across as many as four functions**, and finds buying-group consensus strongly associated with higher-quality deals.

Source:
https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process

Admonk implication:
- do not design GTM around one mythical decision maker;
- the product story must make sense to the business champion, IT/security, finance/procurement and executive stakeholders together.

## External source 2 — McKinsey

McKinsey's enterprise technology GTM research reports:
- around **six stakeholders** in average technology buying decisions;
- roughly **90% of value driven by business outcomes rather than IT priorities**;
- technology buying increasingly involves a broad stakeholder group rather than IT alone.

Source:
https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/the-digital-reinvention-of-enterprise-tech-go-to-market

Admonk implication:
- lead with measurable business outcomes;
- still support technical/security buying criteria;
- expect a hybrid business + technology buying unit.

## Scenario A — CIO / IT-led enterprise platform buyer

### Model
Target large enterprises first. CIO/CTO/IT transformation owns the buying motion and selects Admonk as a strategic platform; departments adopt modules afterward.

### Strengths
- one senior sponsor;
- larger initial contracts;
- easier platform/governance conversation;
- natural fit for integration/security architecture.

### Challenge
- conflicts with specialist-first M3-01;
- longer enterprise sales cycle;
- asks customer to buy broad platform value before proving specialist value;
- department users may experience a top-down tool rather than a solution to their own problem;
- increases implementation/procurement burden early.

## Scenario B — Business-outcome owner leads; cross-functional group approves

### Model
Entry buyer/champion is the leader accountable for the specialist product's outcome.

Examples:
- Marketing Hub → CMO / Head of Marketing / Marketing Operations leader;
- Support product → Head of Customer Service / Support / CX;
- future specialist product → corresponding business owner.

Buying unit then includes, as relevant:
- IT / enterprise architecture;
- security/privacy;
- finance/procurement;
- data/integration owner;
- executive sponsor.

Company-wide Jarvis/platform expansion later raises CIO/CTO/COO/executive importance.

### Strengths
- directly matches M3-01 specialist-first strategy;
- value owner has a concrete problem and KPI;
- smaller initial adoption scope;
- clearer land-and-expand motion;
- platform governance still satisfies IT/security;
- executive buyer becomes more important as scope expands.

### Challenge
- requires messaging for multiple stakeholders;
- departmental purchase can stall if IT/security is engaged too late;
- portfolio can fragment if expansion is not unified through Admonk One/Jarvis.

## Synthesis

Choose **Scenario B**.

Target organization archetype should be based primarily on complexity, not headcount:

> **A multi-team organization with meaningful operational workflows, multiple systems/data sources, governance/security needs, and at least one department with a measurable problem that an Admonk specialist product can solve.**

Early ICP signals:
- multiple departments/functions;
- several SaaS/provider systems;
- repeated manual/fragmented workflows;
- need for visibility/governance;
- clear departmental KPI owner;
- enough integration/process complexity for the shared platform to create expansion value.

Do not lock a universal employee-count floor yet. Validate size/budget thresholds later from pricing, sales-cycle and pilot evidence.

## Buying-unit model

### Champion / economic value owner
Business/function leader responsible for the initial problem and KPI.

### Technical / risk validators
IT, security, data/integration and privacy as needed.

### Commercial approvers
Finance/procurement according to deal size/process.

### Executive sponsor
Optional at first specialist-product purchase; increasingly important for multi-product/Jarvis Company Intelligence expansion.

## Recommended lock

> **M3-02 — Business-led, Cross-Functional Buying Unit**
>
> Admonk targets organizations with sufficient workflow/system/governance complexity for a shared platform to create value, rather than defining the ICP primarily by employee count.
>
> The initial buying champion is normally the business/function leader accountable for the specialist product's outcome.
>
> Technology, security, data, finance and procurement stakeholders are treated as part of the buying group—not late-stage blockers.
>
> CIO/CTO/COO/executive sponsorship becomes progressively more important as the customer expands from one specialist product into a multi-product Admonk deployment and Jarvis Company Intelligence.
>
> The GTM motion is therefore:
> **business outcome entry → cross-functional validation → specialist-product proof → platform expansion → executive/company intelligence.**
>
> Exact firmographic thresholds, pricing bands and enterprise segmentation remain evidence questions for later commercial planning.

**Recommendation:** LOCKED — Scenario B.
