# FOUNDATION-M3-13 — Product-Family Naming / Legacy AI Suite Disposition

**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** LOCKED — OWNER SELECTED SCENARIO B; MASTERBRAND NAME REMAINS TEMPORARY  
**Milestone:** FOUNDATION-M3 — Main Product Master Plan  
**Research depth:** Deep decision research  
**Implementation authority:** None

## 1. Topic overview

This gate exists because the product architecture evolved faster than the original umbrella terminology.

Older canonical coordination material still uses:
- **Admonk AI Suite** as the family/coordination name;
- AI-SUITE.md, AI-SUITE-MANIFEST.yaml and AI-SUITE-REPOSITORY-MAP.md;
- a cross-repository project/coordination concept called **Admonk AI Suite**;
- historical **Corporate AI Assistant** terminology.

The newer locked Foundation architecture now says:
- Admonk commercially leads with **specialist products**;
- the family promise is **Specialist products. One connected platform. One intelligent experience.**;
- the shared platform/foundation is **Admonk Platform**;
- the shared customer administration/setup/health destination is **Admonk One**;
- **Jarvis** is the intelligent experience across products;
- product-scoped Jarvis is included in each specialist product;
- **Jarvis Company Intelligence** is the cross-product add-on;
- there is no second company brain/assistant architecture;
- Marketing Hub and Support remain independent specialist products with durable identities;
- AI is embedded throughout the family and is not itself the sole customer outcome.

The open question is:

> Should “Admonk AI Suite” remain the official product-family/customer-facing umbrella, or should Admonk retire that label and use the Admonk masterbrand + named products/platform/Jarvis architecture directly?

This affects customer comprehension, future catalog extensibility, brand hierarchy, sales language, navigation/product switcher labels, documentation, repository metadata, future product naming, Jarvis positioning and migration of historical terminology.

The answer must avoid two opposite failures:

1. **Too much umbrella naming:** customers meet Admonk, Admonk AI Suite, Admonk Platform, Admonk One, Jarvis and the specialist product names.
2. **Too little family coherence:** every product appears unrelated and the shared platform/Jarvis advantage becomes invisible.

## 2. Internal evidence

### 2.1 Legacy AI Suite model

docs/AI-SUITE.md currently describes **Admonk AI Suite** as a coordination layer for specialist products and explicitly says it is not one giant application.

docs/AI-SUITE-MANIFEST.yaml still declares:
- suite name: Admonk AI Suite;
- commercial composition: composable product family;
- sellable in parts;
- products include Jarvis Company Intelligence, Support Platform and Marketing Hub.

This terminology was useful during the earlier discovery stage because Admonk needed a name for umbrella coordination before the shared Product Platform Foundation and Jarvis architecture were fully defined.

### 2.2 Why the old label is now semantically weaker

M3-01 through M3-12 have made the architecture more precise.

Current layers are:

**Admonk**
- company/masterbrand/product family;

**Admonk Platform**
- shared platform/foundation capabilities included underneath the family;

**Admonk One**
- shared administration/setup/health surface;

**Specialist products**
- independently valuable domain products such as Marketing Hub and Support;

**Jarvis**
- one intelligent experience embedded in each specialist product;

**Jarvis Company Intelligence**
- separately entitled cross-product operating lens/add-on.

Calling the whole structure an **AI Suite** creates three risks:

1. It makes AI sound like the primary category even though M3-01 intentionally leads with specialist business outcomes.
2. It competes conceptually with Jarvis, which is now the named AI/intelligent experience.
3. “Suite” can imply a bundled package, while M3-06 says products are independently purchasable and bundles merely compose atomic SKUs.

### 2.3 Existing naming debt

Legacy terminology exists in filenames, manifests, repository-map docs, skills/coordination naming, historical project-board language and Corporate AI Assistant references.

A naming decision therefore needs a **disposition/migration rule**, not just a preferred marketing phrase.

## 3. Authoritative source 1 — Microsoft 365 + Microsoft Copilot

### Primary evidence

Microsoft uses **Microsoft 365** as a broad umbrella subscription/product-family identity covering apps, intelligent cloud services and security.

Microsoft explicitly says the rename from **Office 365** to **Microsoft 365** was intended to better reflect the wider range of features and benefits beyond the older Office framing.

Within that umbrella:
- Word remains Word;
- Excel remains Excel;
- PowerPoint remains PowerPoint;
- Outlook remains Outlook;
- Teams remains Teams;
- **Copilot** is a named AI assistant/capability that works across those apps.

Microsoft's current Copilot material describes Copilot as built into and connected across existing Microsoft 365 applications rather than renaming the entire family “AI Suite.”

Primary sources:
- https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/what-is-microsoft-365
- https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/in-apps-for-work

### What Microsoft is optimizing for

Microsoft separates three identity jobs:

1. **Master/family identity** — Microsoft 365.
2. **Durable specialist application identity** — Word, Excel, Teams, Outlook, etc.
3. **Cross-application AI identity** — Copilot.

This lets AI become pervasive without forcing AI into every product-family label.

### Lesson for Admonk

Microsoft supports the argument that Admonk should not make “AI” the umbrella category merely because AI is deeply integrated.

A broad family name should survive capability shifts.

If AI becomes expected infrastructure rather than novelty, a family name built around “AI” ages faster than the underlying products.

### Counter-lesson

Microsoft also demonstrates that a **named family umbrella** can be highly useful.

Microsoft 365 provides one commercial family, shared account/admin coherence and a recognizable umbrella above individual apps.

Therefore the lesson is not “never use a family name.”

It is:

> Use a family name that describes the durable customer relationship/value boundary, while letting AI have its own cross-product identity.

For Admonk, the question is whether **Admonk itself** is already sufficient as that family identity, or whether “Admonk AI Suite” adds useful clarity.

## 4. Authoritative source 2 — Atlassian Platform + Rovo

### Primary evidence

Atlassian presents:
- **Atlassian** as the masterbrand/platform family;
- individually named apps such as Jira, Confluence, Loom and Jira Service Management;
- **Collections** that bundle selected apps around outcomes;
- a shared Atlassian platform;
- **Rovo** as the AI-powered experience/capability spanning apps, search, chat, agents and organizational knowledge.

Atlassian's current platform and product catalog show individual products, collections and Rovo side by side. Rovo can operate across Jira, Confluence, Loom and connected tools while the specialist application identities remain intact.

Primary sources:
- https://www.atlassian.com/platform
- https://www.atlassian.com/software/rovo

### What Atlassian is optimizing for

Atlassian does not require one additional permanent “AI Suite” label between the company masterbrand and the apps.

It uses:
1. masterbrand/platform;
2. specialist apps;
3. optional/packaged Collections;
4. Rovo as cross-product AI.

This creates room for multiple commercial combinations without changing what each specialist product is called.

### Lesson for Admonk

This is structurally close to the model M3 has already locked:

**Atlassian → platform → apps → Rovo**

maps conceptually to:

**Admonk → Admonk Platform / Admonk One → specialist products → Jarvis**

The analogy is not exact, but it demonstrates that:
- family coherence does not require “Suite” in the name;
- AI can have a strong named identity without defining the whole family;
- bundles can be named later around customer outcomes rather than defining the entire architecture.

### Counter-lesson

Atlassian has very strong masterbrand recognition.

Admonk is earlier.

A new customer may need more explanation than “Admonk” alone currently provides.

Removing “AI Suite” means positioning copy must work harder to communicate what Admonk is until the brand becomes familiar.

That is a real cost.

## 5. What the two sources agree on

### 5.1 The family identity should outlive one technology wave

Microsoft broadened beyond “Office.”
Atlassian organizes around work/products/platform rather than calling the whole family an AI suite.

### 5.2 Specialist products deserve durable identities

The product name tells the customer what operating surface or outcome they are buying.

### 5.3 Cross-product AI can have its own identity

Copilot and Rovo are recognizable AI layers without replacing every product's name.

### 5.4 Bundling and product identity are different questions

A bundle/collection can package products commercially without becoming the permanent architectural umbrella.

### 5.5 Brand hierarchy should reduce, not multiply, concepts

The strongest systems make each name do one job.

## 6. Scenario A — Keep “Admonk AI Suite” as the Official Family Umbrella

### Naming architecture

**Admonk**
→ **Admonk AI Suite**
→ Admonk Platform
→ Admonk One
→ Marketing Hub
→ Support Platform
→ Jarvis / Jarvis Company Intelligence

“Admonk AI Suite” remains:
- the customer-facing family name;
- portfolio/project umbrella;
- suite documentation identity;
- sales description for the connected family.

### Strengths

**Immediate category signal**
- “AI Suite” quickly tells a new customer that the family is AI-powered.

**Historical continuity**
- existing docs, manifests, skills and coordination language need less migration.

**Clear portfolio umbrella**
- it provides a label for “all Admonk products together.”

**Commercial bundling language**
- sales can easily say one product from the Admonk AI Suite or a full-suite deployment.

### Challenge Scenario A

**It adds a layer without a unique job.**

Customers may now encounter:
- Admonk;
- Admonk AI Suite;
- Admonk Platform;
- Admonk One;
- Jarvis;
- Jarvis Company Intelligence;
- specialist product names.

That is excessive naming density.

**“AI” conflicts with specialist-first positioning.**

M3-01 explicitly says Admonk should not position primarily as one giant generic AI platform.

“AI Suite” pushes the master narrative back toward technology instead of specialist outcomes.

**It competes with Jarvis.**

If Jarvis is the named intelligent experience, AI Suite and Jarvis both try to signal the AI layer.

**“Suite” implies packaged unity.**

M3-06 says products are independently purchasable and bundles compose SKUs. “Suite” can imply the bundle is the primary product.

**Future-proofing risk.**

Admonk's value can increasingly include deterministic automation, integrations, governance, analytics, evidence, collaboration, data infrastructure and AI. Calling everything “AI Suite” may become too narrow.

**Differentiation risk.**

“AI Suite” is generic category language. It describes a technology class more than unique brand meaning.

### Scenario A conclusion

Useful during early discovery and internal coordination.

Increasingly redundant and strategically misaligned as the product architecture becomes clearer.

## 7. Scenario B — Retire “AI Suite” as an Active Product-Family Brand; Use Admonk as the Family

### Naming architecture

**Admonk**
- masterbrand and product-family identity.

**Admonk Platform**
- shared platform/foundation layer;
- usually infrastructure, not a separate specialist product customers must understand deeply.

**Admonk One**
- customer-facing administration, setup, health and shared-control destination.

**Specialist products**
- Marketing Hub;
- Support product;
- future specialist products that pass the M3-03 boundary test.

**Jarvis**
- shared intelligent experience included inside specialist products.

**Jarvis Company Intelligence**
- cross-product add-on / Company-Executive Operating Lens.

**Bundles / Collections**
- created and named later only when commercial evidence justifies a customer-facing package.

### What happens to “Admonk AI Suite”

It becomes:

**LEGACY / HISTORICAL COORDINATION TERMINOLOGY**

Rules:
- no new customer-facing product, website, sales or navigation surface uses “Admonk AI Suite” as the canonical family name;
- no new Foundation decision uses AI Suite as the current architecture label;
- historical docs remain available for provenance;
- legacy docs receive a clear supersession banner pointing to the current Foundation/Product Master Plan;
- filenames may remain temporarily for stable references, then be migrated deliberately with redirects/reference updates where useful;
- legacy manifests, skills and project-board labels are migrated to current Admonk/Foundation naming;
- “AI Suite” may appear only when discussing the historical architecture phase;
- existing product/repository identities are not destructively renamed merely to make everything uniform.

### Customer-facing hierarchy

A customer should normally understand only:

1. **Admonk** — the family/company they buy from.
2. **The specialist product(s)** they use.
3. **Jarvis** — the intelligent experience.
4. **Admonk One** — where authorized admins manage the organization/products.

Admonk Platform can remain visible when needed for enterprise architecture/security/integration discussions, but it does not need to become another daily destination.

### Example

A Marketing-only customer:

> **Admonk Marketing Hub**  
> with Jarvis  
> administered through Admonk One.

A multi-product customer:

> **Admonk**  
> Marketing Hub + Support  
> Jarvis in each product  
> optional Jarvis Company Intelligence across both  
> Admonk One for shared administration.

No additional “AI Suite” explanation is required.

### Strengths

**Strong alignment with M3-01**
- product outcome leads;
- AI remains a multiplier rather than the category that swallows every product.

**Cleaner brand architecture**
- Admonk = family;
- product name = specialist outcome;
- Jarvis = intelligence;
- Admonk One = administration;
- Platform = shared foundation.

**Better future extensibility**
- a future product does not need to justify whether it sounds sufficiently “AI” to belong.

**Commercial flexibility**
- Admonk can sell one product, several products, Jarvis Company Intelligence or future bundles without “Suite” implying all-or-nothing purchase.

**Jarvis becomes clearer**
- there is one named intelligent experience.

**Less conceptual duplication**
- one naming layer disappears from onboarding, navigation, sales, documentation and training.

### Challenge Scenario B

**Loss of immediate category explanation.**

“Admonk” alone does not explain the product category to a first-time buyer.

Mitigation:
- positioning/tagline/copy should explain the promise rather than using an architecture noun.
- M3-01 already supplies a stronger family promise: **Specialist products. One connected platform. One intelligent experience.**

**Migration work.**

Docs and project metadata currently use AI Suite terminology.

Mitigation:
- treat this as a controlled nomenclature migration, not a mass rename.
- use supersession metadata first and rename only where it improves active navigation.

**Potential ambiguity between Admonk and Admonk Platform.**

Mitigation:
- customer language defaults to **Admonk** for the family and product names for purchases;
- use **Admonk Platform** primarily in shared technical/enterprise capability discussions.

**Admonk One may look like another sellable product.**

Mitigation:
- always describe Admonk One as the included admin/setup/health experience, not a separate specialist SKU.

**Support product naming is not fully normalized.**

Mitigation:
- do not make M3-13 solve every specialist product name;
- product-specific final naming stays with its owning product/GTM work.

## 9. Challenge against the locked Admonk plan

### M3-01 — Specialist-first, platform-backed

Scenario B directly matches this.

The first customer noun after Admonk can be the specialist product, not “AI Suite.”

### M3-03 — outcome-owned catalog

Scenario B avoids turning “AI Suite” into an accidental catalog object.

### M3-04 — Jarvis architecture

Scenario B gives Jarvis uncontested ownership of the intelligent-experience identity.

### M3-05 — standalone value

A standalone Marketing Hub customer does not need to conceptually buy into a full suite.

### M3-06 — atomic SKU packaging

Scenario B better supports independent products and optional bundles.

### M3-07 — Admonk One

Admonk One remains clearly an included admin destination rather than a sub-product beneath “AI Suite.”

### M3-08 — shared family shell

The shell can display Admonk + current product + Jarvis/admin utilities without a separate suite-layer navigation label.

### M3-09 — federated domains

The naming hierarchy mirrors the architecture: family coherence without technical collapse.

### M3-10 / M3-11 / M3-12

Lifecycle, health and release administration live naturally under Admonk One/Admonk rather than an “AI Suite” abstraction.

### Cost / migration discipline

Scenario B should **not** trigger a destructive rename of every historical file/repository immediately.

The correct migration is:
1. lock current nomenclature;
2. mark historical AI Suite artifacts legacy/superseded;
3. update active indexes, handover, manifests and skills;
4. migrate filenames only when useful and safe;
5. preserve historical provenance and references;
6. do not rename specialist repositories merely for cosmetic consistency.

## 10. Admonk synthesis

The Microsoft pattern demonstrates why broad family identity should describe a durable customer value boundary while AI can remain a cross-product capability.

The Atlassian pattern demonstrates that a strong masterbrand, specialist apps, shared platform, optional collections and named AI experience can coexist without adding an “AI Suite” layer.

For the architecture Admonk has now locked, the strongest synthesis is:

> **Admonk is the family. Products deliver specialist outcomes. Jarvis is the intelligence. Admonk One administers the family.**

“Admonk AI Suite” was useful as a discovery/coordination label.

It has now completed that job.

Keeping it as a permanent product-family brand would preserve historical terminology at the cost of making the future architecture harder to explain.

## 12. Owner clarification — working name only

The owner accepted Scenario B with an explicit naming constraint:

> **“Admonk” is a temporary working name.**

Therefore M3-13 locks the **brand architecture**, not the permanent commercial name.

What is locked:
- there is one family/masterbrand identity;
- specialist products remain named outcome products underneath it;
- Jarvis remains the named intelligent experience;
- the shared administration experience remains a separate named role/surface;
- “AI Suite” is retired as the active family architecture label;
- future bundles do not become the permanent family identity by default.

What is **not** locked:
- the final permanent family/masterbrand name;
- whether the eventual replacement for “Admonk” keeps, changes or eliminates the current wordmark;
- final naming of any still-unresolved specialist product;
- final customer-facing name of the shared administration surface if later brand research recommends a rename.

Until a dedicated naming/GTM exercise selects the permanent name, **“Admonk” functions only as the canonical working placeholder used to keep architecture/docs coherent.**

Principle:

> **Lock the naming architecture now; defer the permanent name until evidence supports it.**
