# Product Supervisor Research Tracks

Research proceeds in parallel where dependencies allow. Adoption remains blocked until evidence and human approval exist.

## Track A — Product quality and differentiation
Problem validation, scope, premium quality, differentiation, product evidence.

## Track B — Design system and UX
Design Foundation, interaction quality, accessibility, content design, responsive behavior.

## Track C — Engineering simplicity
Abstraction, dependencies, maintainability, architecture proportionality, reuse.

## Track D — Security, privacy and accessibility
Secure development, authorization, privacy, accessibility requirements and evidence.

## Track E — AI autonomy and evaluation
Agent authority, tool permissions, prompt injection, evaluation, regression, cost.

## Track F — Delivery, observability and recovery
Testing, CI/release, monitoring, rollback, backup, incident readiness.

## Track G — Tooling, connectors and capability architecture
Capability gaps, feedback loops, tool/technology candidates, connector coverage, cost/vendor impact.

## Coordination rule

Tracks may influence each other.
A practical tooling constraint may invalidate a proposed doctrine.
A doctrine principle may rule out a tool.
Record the interaction rather than forcing a linear answer.
