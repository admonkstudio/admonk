# Admonk Product Quality Principles

**Status:** APPROVED DOCTRINE  
**Approved:** 2026-09-26  
**Owner:** Admonk Studio  
**Research basis:** `research/synthesis/R002-premium-product-quality.md`  
**Sources:** SRC-QUAL-001, SRC-QUAL-002

## Purpose

Define what "premium product quality" means for Admonk without reducing quality to either checklist compliance or subjective polish.

## 1. Universal quality floor + concentrated craft

> **Every meaningful surface must meet the relevant correctness, trust, usability, accessibility and completeness baseline. The highest craft investment goes to critical, frequent and differentiating journeys.**

Admonk does not use "premium" to justify equal polish everywhere.

## 2. Quality floor

Every meaningful product area must meet the relevant evidence-backed baseline for its context, including as applicable:
- functional correctness;
- reliability and recovery;
- security and privacy;
- usability and accessibility;
- responsive behavior and performance;
- data integrity;
- compatibility/interoperability;
- maintainability/change safety;
- operational observability appropriate to maturity.

A visually impressive surface that fails the relevant floor is not premium.

## 3. Craft and coherence

Above the floor, premium quality requires intentional experience:
- complete important states;
- clear hierarchy;
- predictable behavior where predictability builds trust;
- product-appropriate density;
- coherent content and interactions;
- removal of avoidable friction and papercuts;
- attention to detail;
- calm, trustworthy behavior under non-ideal conditions.

## 4. Concentrated craft

Invest the strongest design and implementation craft in:
- the core value moment;
- primary/high-frequency workflows;
- onboarding and activation;
- trust-sensitive or consequential actions;
- product-defining interactions;
- areas that create meaningful brand/product differentiation.

Secondary areas still meet the quality floor. They do not need identical creative investment.

## 5. Product-specific expression

Premium does not mean:
- one visual style;
- luxury aesthetics;
- more animation;
- more decoration;
- maximal polish everywhere;
- identical interface density;
- one component layout.

Products may express quality differently according to users, domain, brand and risk.

## 6. Complete-state principle

A meaningful surface must intentionally handle the non-ideal states that can materially occur, such as:
- loading;
- empty;
- error;
- success;
- disabled;
- permission denied;
- partial/stale data;
- long/unexpected content;
- responsive/input-mode behavior;
- destructive confirmation/recovery.

## 7. Evidence before quality claims

For every material quality claim ask:

> **What proves this?**

Evidence may include:
- automated tests;
- browser evidence;
- accessibility checks;
- performance measurements;
- security review;
- recovery tests;
- user observation/feedback;
- product analytics;
- visual/state review;
- design-system conformance;
- incident/bug evidence;
- expert judgment where the quality is inherently qualitative.

Judgment is valid.
Unexamined judgment is not enough.

## 8. AI-assisted quality

AI may accelerate production and help detect defects, inconsistencies and regressions.

AI must not become the sole authority for:
- appropriateness;
- coherence;
- product differentiation;
- trustworthiness;
- final design judgment.

Speed gained through AI should not reduce:
- verification;
- state completeness;
- accessibility;
- ownership;
- product-specific reasoning.

## 9. Economic proportionality

Quality investment should be proportional to:
- user importance;
- business importance;
- frequency;
- risk;
- differentiation value;
- reversibility;
- support/operational impact.

Avoid polishing low-value areas while critical workflows remain weak.

## Final rule

> **Premium software earns trust everywhere and concentrates exceptional craft where it matters most.**
