# Admonk Image Direction

Use this skill whenever creating, selecting, critiquing, or refining imagery for a web experience.

The goal is not to create a visually impressive image in isolation.

The goal is to create an image that strengthens the **website, brand, message, and experience**.

---

# 1. Load Context Selectively

Read:

* `docs/IMAGE-DIRECTION.md`

when the task involves important imagery.

Also read:

* `docs/DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md`

when the image affects a major composition or page concept.

Read:

* `docs/MOTION-LANGUAGE.md`

when the image will animate or needs layered assets.

Read:

* `docs/ADMONK-BUSINESS.md`

when the image is for Admonk itself or business positioning matters.

Do not load all reference documents for a minor image adjustment.

---

# 2. Start With the Image's Job

Before creating anything, determine:

1. What does this image need to communicate?
2. What should the viewer feel?
3. Where will it appear?
4. What content will sit around it?
5. Is it static or animated?
6. What must remain visible after responsive cropping?

Never begin with:

> "Make something cool."

Begin with:

> **What is the image doing for the experience?**

---

# 3. Default Direction

Unless the brand requires otherwise, lean toward imagery that feels:

* Cinematic
* Editorial
* Premium
* Realistic
* Art-directed
* Controlled
* Atmospheric
* Sophisticated

Prefer a strong visual idea over a complicated scene.

---

# 4. Website First

Always design the image for its destination.

Consider:

* Aspect ratio
* Text-safe area
* Subject position
* Mobile crop
* Desktop crop
* Motion requirements
* Background contrast
* CTA placement

Do not generate the image first and solve composition problems afterward.

---

# 5. Realism First for AI Imagery

The default AI-image preference is:

> **Photographic realism.**

Prioritize:

* Believable skin
* Realistic materials
* Natural reflections
* Physical lighting
* Coherent perspective
* Correct anatomy
* Realistic depth

Even when the concept is surreal, execution should often remain believable.

Think:

> **Impossible idea, believable photograph.**

---

# 6. Art Direction Before Prompting

For important images, define:

```text id="grth8m"
OBJECTIVE
SUBJECT
COMPOSITION
CAMERA
LIGHTING
MATERIALS
COLOR
MOOD
WEBSITE USE
SAFE AREA
MOTION REQUIREMENTS
AVOID
```

Do not rely on vague directions such as:

> premium futuristic visual

or:

> beautiful AI image.

---

# 7. Composition

Composition matters more than decorative detail.

Decide:

* Where is the subject?
* How large is it?
* Where is the visual weight?
* Where is the negative space?
* What leads the eye?
* What gets cropped?

Prefer controlled, intentional compositions.

Avoid automatic center placement unless the concept calls for it.

---

# 8. Negative Space

Use negative space deliberately.

It can provide:

* Headline space
* CTA space
* Luxury
* Calm
* Hierarchy
* Responsive flexibility

If copy must appear on the image, generate the safe area intentionally.

Do not plan to "find space later."

---

# 9. Camera Thinking

Think like a photographer.

Choose an appropriate visual perspective:

* Close-up
* Macro
* Eye-level
* Low-angle
* High-angle
* Wide environmental
* Telephoto
* Over-the-shoulder
* First-person
* Three-quarter product view

Do not default to generic front-facing product shots.

---

# 10. Lighting

Lighting is a primary design tool.

Define:

* Direction
* Softness
* Contrast
* Temperature
* Accent color
* Shadow depth
* Edge lighting

Preferred approaches may include:

* Soft studio light
* Controlled hard light
* Rim light
* Screen glow
* Low-key cinematic lighting
* Brand-colored edge light

Do not add glow everywhere to make an image feel "tech."

---

# 11. Color

Prefer:

> **Neutral environment + controlled accent**

when appropriate.

However, brand and concept come first.

The visual may legitimately be:

* Dark
* Light
* Monochrome
* Saturated
* Gradient-led
* Warm
* Cool

if the message supports it.

---

# 12. Brand-Colored Light

Whenever possible, consider expressing brand color through:

* Lighting
* Reflection
* Glow
* Surface illumination
* Environmental accents

rather than making every object the brand color.

This often creates a more premium result.

---

# 13. Human Imagery

When people are used, prefer:

* Editorial framing
* Strong crops
* Hands
* Eyes
* Profiles
* Silhouettes
* Gestures
* Partial faces
* Human interaction with products

Avoid generic corporate photography.

Avoid people staring at a laptop simply because the company is digital.

---

# 14. Mockups

Mockups should feel art-directed.

Do not settle for:

> Screenshot + standard phone template + gradient background.

Consider:

* Human interaction
* Environment
* Lighting
* Materials
* Architecture
* Perspective
* Device scale
* Cropping
* Physical context

The mockup should contribute to the brand experience.

---

# 15. Show the Real Work

When a real interface exists:

Prefer using the actual UI.

Use AI for:

* Environment
* Device
* Hand
* Lighting
* Scene
* Perspective

Then preserve accurate interface content when practical.

Do not ask AI to recreate precise UI if the real design is available.

---

# 16. Text and Logos

Do not rely on AI-generated text for:

* Headlines
* Logos
* UI labels
* Product copy
* Brand names

when accuracy matters.

Prefer compositing accurate assets afterward.

---

# 17. Visual Metaphor

For abstract ideas, use simple visual metaphors.

Examples:

### Connection

Paths joining elements.

### Transformation

One form becoming another.

### Automation

One action triggering multiple outcomes.

### Growth

Expansion or progression.

### Identity

A consistent brand language appearing across touchpoints.

The metaphor should be understandable quickly.

---

# 18. Avoid Literal Technology Clichés

For AI and automation, avoid defaulting to:

* Robots
* Circuit brains
* Glowing heads
* Hologram hands
* Binary code
* Generic server rooms
* Blue network nodes

Prefer representing technology through:

* Behavior
* Interface
* Data flow
* Human interaction
* Connection
* Transformation

---

# 19. Materiality

Use materials when they reinforce the brand.

Possible materials include:

* Glass
* Metal
* Paper
* Fabric
* Acrylic
* Rubber
* Chrome
* Stone
* Plastic
* Light

Do not use materials simply because they are trendy.

---

# 20. 3D

3D is appropriate when spatial form helps communicate the idea.

It may be used for:

* Products
* Branded objects
* Visual metaphors
* Interfaces
* Environments
* Material studies

Do not create meaningless abstract 3D objects to fill empty areas.

---

# 21. Image Families

Important projects should not be generated image-by-image without a shared direction.

Maintain consistency through:

* Lighting
* Contrast
* Color treatment
* Camera style
* Materials
* Mood
* Grain
* Cropping

Different scenes should still belong to the same visual world.

---

# 22. Consistency Without Sameness

A project may contain:

```text id="urkl0x"
Portrait
   ↓
Device
   ↓
Architecture
   ↓
Product detail
```

and still feel connected through art direction.

Do not repeat one type of image endlessly.

---

# 23. Portfolio Imagery

Portfolio imagery should create:

### Proof

Show the work accurately.

### Aspiration

Help future clients imagine their own business presented with the same level of quality.

This means portfolio imagery deserves dedicated art direction.

---

# 24. Portfolio Hero

Do not automatically use a homepage screenshot as the case-study hero.

Ask:

> **What single visual best captures the character of this project?**

Possible answers:

* Device scene
* Brand portrait
* Environmental visual
* Full interface
* 3D object
* Cinematic composition

---

# 25. Avoid Mockup Repetition

Do not create a portfolio where every project is:

```text id="435m8v"
phone
phone
phone
phone
phone
```

Vary:

* Phone
* Tablet
* Environment
* Close-up
* Interface
* Signage
* Human interaction
* Architecture
* 3D
* Motion

---

# 26. Motion Preparation

If the visual will animate, prepare for it during creation.

Ask whether the asset needs:

* Transparent background
* Separate foreground
* Separate subject
* Extra crop area
* High resolution
* Layered lighting
* Room for scale
* Alternative aspect ratio

Do not discover motion limitations after finalizing the image.

---

# 27. Responsive Image Direction

Do not assume one composition works everywhere.

A desktop image may require:

> subject right, copy left.

Mobile may require:

> subject centered, copy above.

Use alternate crops or separate generations when necessary.

---

# 28. Reference Analysis

When a reference image is provided, analyze:

* Composition
* Camera
* Light
* Color
* Material
* Mood
* Negative space
* Depth
* Brand relationship

Then apply the principles.

Do not simply reproduce the reference with another logo.

---

# 29. Reference Boards

For major visual directions, combine several references.

For example:

```text id="xy3gfs"
Reference A → lighting
Reference B → composition
Reference C → material
Reference D → camera
```

This creates a new direction rather than imitation.

---

# 30. Iteration

After generation, identify the exact failure.

Examples:

* Subject too large
* Crop too tight
* Lighting too flat
* Accent color too strong
* Background too busy
* Materials too plastic
* Negative space missing
* Perspective wrong

Fix the problem specifically.

Do not regenerate randomly until something looks better.

---

# 31. Quality Inspection

Always inspect important imagery for:

### People

* Hands
* Fingers
* Eyes
* Teeth
* Limbs
* Clothing
* Joints

### Products

* Edges
* Buttons
* Screen geometry
* Symmetry
* Perspective
* Reflections

### Environment

* Repeated structures
* Impossible geometry
* Broken shadows
* Inconsistent light

One obvious AI error can ruin an otherwise premium image.

---

# 32. Restraint

Do not combine every visual technique at once.

Avoid:

```text id="nar8mn"
glass
+ chrome
+ glow
+ particles
+ fog
+ gradient
+ 3D
+ reflections
```

unless the concept truly needs them.

Prefer fewer techniques executed extremely well.

---

# 33. Background Discipline

Backgrounds should support the subject.

Do not add random:

* Furniture
* Light sources
* Architecture
* Props
* Shapes

just to fill the scene.

A simple controlled background often feels more expensive.

---

# 34. Minimalism

Minimal imagery can still feel highly art-directed.

One:

* Hand
* Device
* Object
* Face
* Material

can carry the entire composition.

Complexity is not the same as sophistication.

---

# 35. Image Review

Before approval, ask:

## Purpose

* What does this communicate?

## Brand

* Does this belong to this business?

## Composition

* Is there one dominant visual idea?
* Is negative space intentional?

## Realism

* Are materials and anatomy believable?

## Website

* Does the crop work?
* Does text have space?
* Does mobile work?

## Quality

* Does this feel expensive?
* Does anything immediately look AI-generated?

## Originality

* Is the visual idea deliberate?
* Or is it simply visually impressive?

---

# 36. Final Principle

The goal is not:

> **Generate beautiful images.**

The goal is:

> **Create visual assets that make the business feel more valuable and make the website experience stronger.**

The final test is:

> **Does this help the client see their business the way they want the world to see it?**
